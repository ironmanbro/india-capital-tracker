About and methodology

What this site is, where its numbers come from, and how far to trust them.

What it is

Indian Capital Tracker follows foreign money moving into and out of India: portfolio flows, exchange deals, investor registrations, fund launches and exits, outbound investment by Indian companies, GIFT City entities, and the SEBI, RBI and IFSCA rules that govern them. It is free to use.

Source grades

Items on the site carry a grade tag.

  • A: published by an exchange, depository or regulator (NSE, BSE, NSDL, SEBI, RBI, CCIL, IFSCA, DPIIT). Shown as published, or converted or summed by this site.
  • C: reported in the news or other secondary sources and not independently verified. Each links to its publisher. Check the original before relying on it.

Datasets and sources

  • FPI net investment by day, month and year: NSDL FPI Monitor.
  • Bulk and block deals, FII/DII cash flows, corporate announcements: NSE and BSE files.
  • Registered FPIs: the NSDL register. GIFT City entities: IFSCA registers.
  • FPI debt limits and utilisation: CCIL. Outbound investment (ODI): RBI data.
  • Rules and circulars: SEBI, RBI, IFSCA, CCI and DPIIT publications. Plain-English summaries are written to orient the reader; the official document is the authority.
  • Reported deals and developments: news feeds, classified automatically and screened.

Refresh

News and exchange data are collected hourly. News is reviewed once a day, at 9 am IST, before it appears as a story; exchange deals appear within the hour. Each source publishes on its own schedule, so every panel shows the date its data runs to.

Exchange rates

Rupee conversions use FBIL's published USD/INR reference rate, with its date shown in the footer. If that rate is more than 14 days old, the European Central Bank reference rate is used instead and the footer says so. Figures that NSDL reports directly in US dollars are shown as published. The default currency is the rupee; you can switch it at the top of any page.

Limits you should know about

  • Automated classification of news can be wrong: amounts, parties and dates may be incomplete or out of date.
  • "Foreign" investor status in exchange deals is judged from the client's name, so it can be wrong.
  • Some history is limited, as each data panel states.
  • Calendar-year and fiscal-year totals differ; each figure says which it is.
