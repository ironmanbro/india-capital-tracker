Rulebook: FPI, FDI and FVCI

FPI: foreign portfolio investors

Foreign funds, banks, insurers and others buying listed Indian shares, bonds and similar securities without taking control. Regulated by SEBI, with RBI setting the foreign-exchange side.

In short

  • Registration: through a Designated Depository Participant (DDP, usually a custodian bank) under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019. Category I covers lower-risk investors such as governments, central banks and pension funds. Category II covers the rest, such as most funds and individuals.
  • Equity limit: one FPI, together with its group, may hold less than 10% of a company's paid-up equity. All foreign portfolio holdings together are capped at the company's sectoral limit. Passing 10% means selling down or treating the holding as FDI within the time the rules allow.
  • Debt: FPIs invest through the general route, the Voluntary Retention Route (VRR) and the Fully Accessible Route (FAR) for government bonds, within limits set by RBI and SEBI. The tracker shows the current limit use.
  • Disclosure and KYC: custodians and DDPs run KYC and beneficial-ownership checks. FPIs above SEBI's size or concentration thresholds must disclose more about who ultimately owns them.
  • Newer routes: SEBI has introduced the SWAGAT-FI framework for trusted foreign investors, listed in the latest changes below.

FDI: foreign direct investment

A non-resident taking a lasting stake in an Indian company or LLP. Governed by the FEMA Non-debt Instruments Rules, 2019, the government's FDI policy from DPIIT, and its Press Notes, with RBI handling reporting.

In short

  • Two routes: the automatic route needs no prior government approval. The government route needs approval through the relevant ministry. Which one applies depends on the sector.
  • Sector caps and bans: each sector has a maximum foreign share, and some activities, such as lotteries, gambling and chit funds, are closed to foreign investment.
  • Land-border countries: an investor from a country sharing a land border with India, or whose beneficial owner is a citizen of one, can invest only through the Government route, and a later change in beneficial ownership needs prior approval. This began with Press Note 3 of 2020 and was restated by Press Note 2 of 2026 (15 March 2026), which also ties beneficial ownership to the PML Rules and requires investors not needing approval to report under a DPIIT procedure. Pakistan-linked investors are limited further.
  • Pricing and reporting: shares are subject to pricing guidelines. Issues and transfers to non-residents are reported to RBI, for example on the FC-GPR form within 30 days of allotment and the FC-TRS form within 60 days of a transfer. Check the current timelines in the official rules.
  • Link to FPI: an FPI that crosses the 10% limit moves from portfolio to direct investment rules.

FVCI: foreign venture capital investors

Overseas venture capital and private funds that register with SEBI to invest in Indian start-ups, venture capital undertakings and venture funds. Governed by the SEBI (Foreign Venture Capital Investors) Regulations, 2000.

In short

  • Registration: with SEBI through a DDP. Registered FVCIs can invest in unlisted companies and venture funds that ordinary FPIs cannot.
  • Treatment: FVCIs are generally outside the 10% FPI limit and get relaxations on pricing and holding periods under the FEMA rules. Check the Schedule that applies to your structure.
  • Reporting: SEBI has set reporting requirements and operating guidelines for FVCIs and their DDPs, listed below.
  • Recent changes: SEBI amended the regulations in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and SWAGAT-FI lets eligible investors register as an FPI and an FVCI together. See the latest changes below.
